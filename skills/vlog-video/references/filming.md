# Filming: what to bring back

The edit can only use what was filmed. This page is what the director reads before a trip; make them a short
version for the phone with the trip's shot list.

## On the trip
- Film each place from the shot list: a wide shot that shows where you are, the thing itself, a detail, and
  something moving (people, water, a train). Ten seconds or more per shot, held still at the start and the end.
- **Keep the subject in the middle third** of a horizontal shot when the channel also makes vertical videos: a 9:16
  short is cropped from that middle strip. If the channel makes only vertical videos, film upright.
- **A recap every evening**, facing the camera or just into the microphone: where you went, what surprised you,
  what went wrong, what you would tell a friend. **Recaps are the main narration**: a vlog is told in them.
- Anything said on site is a bonus, never a duty. No spoken notes for the editor and no "signboard first, read the
  name aloud" shots: they end up in the cut and under the background sound. Film a signboard silently when it is
  worth showing.
- The place's own sound matters: let a few shots run with nobody talking (a market, rain, a platform
  announcement). The story plan uses them as pauses.
- The itinerary may change freely. Skipped places need no explanation; sorting afterwards handles it.
- After the trip, put the footage into day / place folders as far as you can. What you cannot place, Claude places
  (footage-only.md).

## Sound
- The same microphone for recaps and for anything re-recorded later, in a quiet room, 15–20 cm from the mouth.
- Outdoors, a wind cover on the microphone, always. Wind cannot be removed afterwards.
- Check the level once per trip with a ten-second test: speech peaks around −12 dB, never touching the top.

## Camera (any camera with manual settings)
Make two or three saved presets and stop thinking about settings on the trip:

| Preset | For | Typical settings |
|---|---|---|
| Still / talking | tripod shots, buildings, talking to the camera, indoors | 4K 25p or 30p, shutter about twice the frame rate (1/50, 1/60), continuous autofocus with face priority |
| Walking / moving | walking shots, out of a window | 1080p 50p or 60p (smoother, and steadier when slowed), shutter 1/100–1/125, stabilisation on |
| Bright sun | outdoors at midday without an ND filter | aperture priority so the camera shortens the shutter, smaller aperture |

- White balance fixed on Daylight outdoors (colours match from shot to shot); automatic indoors.
- No log or flat picture profile unless the director grades themselves: the edit only levels brightness, it does
  not grade.
- Set the camera's clock to local time, to the minute: footage is sorted by time.
- Zebra or highlight warning on; faces and bright walls must not blow out. Exposure is fixed on site, never in the
  edit.
- Before the trip, ask for a ten-second test clip in each preset and look at it: exposure, focus, sound level,
  frame rate, and whether the files read on this computer.

## Phone only
A phone is enough for a whole vlog. What changes:
- **Decide the shape before filming.** Upright for vertical videos, sideways for horizontal ones. If the channel
  makes both, film sideways with the subject in the middle (a vertical short can be cropped from it; the reverse
  cannot).
- Resolution 4K at 30 frames when storage allows (a crop to 9:16 stays sharp), else 1080p. Turn HDR video off
  unless every platform they post to shows it correctly: HDR clips look washed out or too bright elsewhere, and
  they do not match non-HDR clips.
- Lock focus and exposure (long press) for a shot that should not pulse; use the main lens, not the digital zoom.
- Hold with two hands, elbows in, and let each shot settle; a small grip or tripod helps more than any setting.
  The pipeline steadies shaky shots, at the cost of a slight crop.
- **Sound is the weak point.** For recaps: indoors, quiet, the phone 20–30 cm from the mouth, not in the hand that
  gestures. A small clip-on or plug-in microphone is the best single purchase. Outdoors, turn your back to the
  wind.
- Phones store the time and the place of every clip: this is what sorts an unsorted trip (footage-only.md).
  Copy the **original files** off the phone (cable, AirDrop "keep originals", or the cloud's "download original"):
  a messaging app or a "share" export shrinks them and removes the time and place.
- Variable frame rate (most phones) is handled: the working copies are made at a constant rate.
- Storage: 4K is about 350 MB a minute. Empty the phone onto a drive each evening when the trip is long.

## Privacy and other people's work
- People who can be recognised and did not agree: avoid filming them close; the privacy check lists every such
  shot before a render and the director decides (leave, blur, cut).
- Tickets, QR codes, car plates, screens with personal data: film them if they tell the story, they can be blurred.
- Museums, performances, music playing in a shop: check the place's rules; music in the background of a shot can
  get a video muted or claimed on some platforms.
- A drone needs the country's permission rules checked before the trip, per place.
