# Story and lines

The director's own recordings are the voice of these videos: what they said on site and in the evening recaps.
Written lines fill only what the recording cannot do. Rules here are tests to check a draft against, never slots to
fill.

Two kinds of video come out of a trip, and a trip may give both:
- a **vlog**: the trip itself, told in order, answering a question about the trip ("is this town worth two days?");
- a **site video**: one place explained, answering a question a viewer has about it. Side stories of the trip (the
  rain, the wrong bus) belong to the vlog, not here.

## Real-viewer interview (step 4, one subagent call for the whole trip)
The persona comes from `CHANNEL.md` (who watches: age, place, what they know). Give it the trip's places and
candidate questions only. Ask: when would you ever think about this? How would you say it to a friend, or type it
into a search box? What would make you say "wait, really?" (ranked)? What do you already believe that may be wrong?
Which candidate questions are real, which invented?
The simplest question a viewer would really ask wins, even if it repeats an earlier video. Then pick how to open.

## Story plan (every video, before any cutting)
The director films freely and wants the story made out of what they brought back. Order and shot choice are what
re-edit rounds are spent on, and they can only be judged after a render, so the order is settled on paper first.
1. **Find the moments in the words, not the footage.** Read every recap and on-site line. List the moments with a
   turn in them: a plan, something going wrong, a surprise, a verdict.
2. **Pick the spine.** One question the video answers at the end, in the viewer's words. A vlog's comes from the
   trip; a site video's from the viewer interview. Three or four moments are the beats; everything else is cut or
   becomes a short bridge. **Never an inventory of everything that was done or seen.**
3. **Each stop is a small loop:** arrive, what it is plus one fact, the director's honest reaction or mishap, a line
   out to the next place.
4. **Breathing room is planned here, not added afterwards.** A person who talks all the time leaves the viewer no
   time to take it in: 5–7 s of the place's own footage and sound at each change of place, 3–4 s after about 40 s
   of unbroken talk. The plan lists the pauses and the longest stretch of talk.
5. **One page, `work/story/<video>.md`**, in the director's language, readable in two minutes: the spine (question
   and answer); the beats in order, what each does and where its voice comes from (which recording, which
   seconds); what is dropped; the opening (a montage of about 10 s cut to its music, or straight on the hook) and
   the ending; the pauses; the music (which track for which part); **the shape and the platforms** (a vertical
   video for a platform with a short limit is planned to that length here, not trimmed later); and at most a
   handful of numbered choices for the director. **Stop.** They approve, reorder or strike out here, where a
   change is free. Only then the edit list.
6. The first cut is built to the plan, with shaky, reused and look-alike shots already fixed. Aim for two review
   rounds: their notes and subtitle edits, one rebuild, their confirmation, then the final.
For a video the director has already edited, the plan describes the cut as it stands and proposes changes; their
edited subtitles are never touched.

A point that another video covers gets about 20 seconds and points there only if that video is already out.

## Lines to re-record (step 5)
Only for: facts that must change, explanations the recording lacks, and joins that do not make sense.
- For each line, find the closest thing the director really said in the trip's transcripts (`work/transcript/`,
  recaps first) and follow its sentence shape and way of thinking. Do not draft in your own feel for the language.
- Sound like the recap: whole connected sentences, spoken words, "you" and small check-ins, their regional way of
  speaking kept exactly.
- Each line can be said in one breath; one new idea per sentence.
- Say the real link (so, because, if… then; "but" only for a real contrast). No "next", "let's", "interestingly".
- Keep every hedge. A fact must stand up in front of someone who knows the place.

**Combined reader check (one subagent):** given only the lines, the recap transcript around them and the persona:
would you ask this here, where do you get lost, does the ending give something new, and which line would the
director not say that way (with a rewrite from a real sentence of theirs)? A fix that adds a caveat goes to the
description unless it prevents a wrong belief that matters.

## Shorts and Reels
A complete little video that makes sense without the long one. One idea. Check the order against: hook (something
viewers have seen, plus the missing piece) → but → so… so… → back to the opening with something new. The answer
appears in full once, at the end. It never ends on a teaser and never names a day ("tomorrow"): it is watched for
months.
- **Length follows the story, then the platform.** Never chop sentences to shorten; remove a whole idea instead.
  If one platform's limit is shorter than the story, plan a shorter story for it, or post it only where it fits.
- A short built from a vlog is one complete moment of the trip. Never stitch lines from different places so that
  they sound like one event.
- The first two seconds carry it on every vertical platform: open on the picture and the line, no greeting, no
  title card.
- Words on screen stay out of the bottom and the right edge, where the platform's buttons sit (platforms.md).
- A vertical video for several platforms is **one picture file with one text per platform** (platforms.md).

## Follow the recording
Subtitles and cuts follow what was actually said. Raise a line only when a fact is wrong or a take is unclear.
Never correct the director's wording or dialect.
